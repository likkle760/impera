01Foundations

The Market — What You're Actually Trading

Before you can win in the markets, you need to understand what moves them. Spoiler: it isn't lines on a chart. It's people, liquidity and timing.

The forex and gold market in one paragraph

Forex is the world's largest market — over $7 trillion changes hands daily across currency pairs like EUR/USD, GBP/USD and USD/JPY. Gold (XAU/USD) sits at the heart of it: a safe-haven asset that reacts violently to inflation data, central bank decisions and geopolitical stress. There is no central exchange; instead, a global network of banks and brokers creates an around-the-clock market, five days a week.

Sessions — the rhythm of the market

The market has a heartbeat. Volume and volatility surge during specific sessions, and die off in between. This matters more than most beginners realise:

This is why every IMPERA system ships with a session filter locked to London + New York. Trading outside these windows means wider spreads, random noise and false signals. The pros simply don't do it.

What actually moves prices

  • Liquidity: price gravitates toward pools of resting orders (stops). Big players hunt these zones — which is exactly why stops belong in logical places, not arbitrary ones.
  • Data releases: CPI, NFP, FOMC and rate decisions create violent moves. Manual traders should stand aside; automated systems manage exposure with spread filters.
  • Sentiment: risk-on days lift AUD, NZD and equities; risk-off days lift USD, CHF and gold. Know the mood before you take a trade.
Hard truth

You will never outsmart the market on information. You can, however, out-discipline 95% of participants by trading a defined plan during high-quality hours only. That alone puts you ahead of the majority.

02Mindset

The IMPERA Philosophy — Precision Over Prediction

Amateurs try to be right. Professionals try to be consistent. Everything we build follows one principle: precision of execution beats accuracy of opinion.

Ask a losing trader what they're working on and they'll say "better predictions." Ask a winning trader and they'll talk about execution quality: better entries, tighter risk, cleaner process. The difference is fundamental. You cannot control where price goes next. You can completely control whether you follow your rules.

The four pillars

1. Rules Before Trades

If a setup doesn't match the written criteria, it doesn't exist. No "it looks like it might work." Either the checklist passes or you stay flat.

2. Risk Is Fixed, Outcomes Are Not

Risk per trade is chosen calmly in advance — never emotionally mid-trade. The market decides winners; you decide exposure.

3. Process Over Outcome

A losing trade taken by the rules is a good trade. A winning trade taken on impulse is a bad trade wearing a disguise.

4. Automate What Works

Once an edge is proven, remove human error from the loop entirely. This is precisely why IMPERA exists as a systems company.

Why the edge is mathematical, not mystical

An edge simply means: over many trades, your average win outweighs your average loss after costs. Example — the IMPERA scalping model targets roughly 1:0.75 reward-to-risk with a high hit-rate profile. At those numbers you remain firmly profitable even winning barely more than half your trades. You do not need to be right often. You need your sizing, exits and frequency to be correct.

Expectancy = (Win% × AvgWin) − (Loss% × AvgLoss)
// e.g. 55% × £150 − 45% × £100 = +£37.50 per trade
// A positive number, repeated hundreds of times, is the whole business model.
IMPERA Rule

Stop asking "will this trade win?" Start asking "does this trade match my system?" One question destroys accounts; the other builds them.

03Toolkit

Technical Analysis — The Six Tools That Matter

These six indicators are the complete toolkit inside IMPERA systems. Learn these deeply and ignore almost everything else.

1. Exponential Moving Averages (EMA)

The EMA weights recent price more heavily, making it responsive to fresh momentum. IMPERA systems use two: a fast EMA (8) and a slow EMA (21). When the fast crosses above the slow, short-term momentum has flipped bullish; below, bearish. On higher timeframes, EMAs double as dynamic support/resistance and trend filters.

  • Price above rising EMAs → look for longs only
  • Price below falling EMAs → look for shorts only
  • EMAs flat and tangled → no-trade zone; stand down

2. Relative Strength Index (RSI)

RSI measures momentum on a 0–100 scale. Above 65–70, buyers are stretched; below 30–35, sellers are exhausted. Crucially, IMPERA uses RSI as a confirmation filter, not a standalone signal: a bullish EMA crossover is only taken when RSI shows room to run or oversold conditions turning up — filtering out exhausted entries into overextended moves.

3. MACD

The Moving Average Convergence Divergence tracks momentum shifts via the gap between two EMAs. Rising histogram = building bullish momentum; falling = bearish. Inside the Gold Bot, MACD confirms trend health on H1 while entries trigger on M15 — a classic multi-timeframe confirmation structure.

4. Stochastic Oscillator

Stochastic locates price within its recent range. Readings under 20 signal oversold; above 80, overbought. Paired with MACD in the Gold Bot, it catches reversal rotations within trending days — buying dips in uptrends rather than tops blindly.

5. Average True Range (ATR)

ATR answers the most important question in trading: "how far does this instrument normally move?" It converts volatility into numbers, letting stops breathe with market conditions instead of sitting at fixed distances that noise routinely clips. The Gold Bot sets both stop-loss and take-profit as multiples of ATR — wider stops in wild markets, tighter in calm ones, identical logic throughout.

6. Bollinger Bands

Bands expand and contract with volatility. Squeezes precede explosions; rides along the band confirm strong trends. The Global Bot uses band breakouts as a third independent strategy — capturing the moment a compressed range finally resolves.

Why only six?

Because indicators describe the same three things — trend, momentum, volatility — in different clothes. Six tools covering all three, combined with strict session timing, is everything. A chart with fifteen indicators is not more informed; it's more conflicted.

04Core System

The IMPERA Strategy — The Exact Entry Model

No secrets held back. These are the precise entry, exit and management rules our own systems execute — the same model taught step-by-step in the mentorship.

The setup (long example)

  1. Trend context: price trading above the rising 21 EMA on M15/H1. Only longs considered.
  2. Trigger: the 8 EMA crosses above the 21 EMA on the M5 chart (fresh cross — bar 1 vs bar 2).
  3. Confirmation: RSI(14) below 35 (pullback exhaustion) or turning up through 50 with momentum.
  4. Session gate: signal occurs between 08:00–20:00 server time (London + New York), Mon–Fri only.
  5. Spread check: spread below the maximum threshold — if the market costs too much to cross, skip it.
Mirror image for shorts

Price below falling 21 EMA + 8 EMA crosses below 21 EMA + RSI above 65 rolling over + valid session + clean spread. Same discipline, opposite direction.

Exits — where trades are won

  • Initial stop: fixed-pips version for majors (default 20), or ATR-based (Gold Bot: SL ≈ 1.5×ATR, TP ≈ 2.5×ATR) so distance adapts to live volatility.
  • Take profit: set at structural/ATR targets — never widened mid-trade on hope.
  • Trailing stop: once profit exceeds ~10 pips, trail by 5-pip steps. Winners get room early, then get locked in — the mechanism that produces lopsided win/loss ratios.
  • Exposure cap: maximum 3 concurrent positions (Scalping Bot), 2 (Gold Bot), 5 (Global Bot). Concentration limits are part of the system, not an afterthought.

05Capital

Risk Management — Your Real Edge

Strategy decides how you win. Risk management decides whether you survive long enough to win at all. This chapter is the most important one in the book.

The 1% commandment

Never risk more than 1% of your account on a single trade (2% absolute ceiling). At 1%, a catastrophic 10-loss streak costs ~9.6% — painful but fully recoverable. At 10% risk, the same streak erases 65% of the account, demanding a +186% climb just to break even. Survival math is brutal and non-negotiable.

Position sizing — the only formula you need

Lot Size = (Account × Risk%) ÷ (Stop Loss in Pips × Pip Value)
// £2,000 account · 1% risk · 20-pip stop · £10/pip per lot (majors)
Lot Size = (£2,000 × 0.01) ÷ (20 × £10) = 0.01 lots

Notice what this means: the stop distance determines the lot size — never the other way round. Choose your invalidation point first (structure/ATR), then let the maths dictate size. This is also how the bots' dynamic lot sizing works: risk stays constant while size scales with balance.

Diversification & correlation

  • EUR/USD and GBP/USD are ~85% correlated — long both simultaneously and you've doubled your real risk, not diversified it.
  • Cap total open risk across all positions (bots enforce this with concurrent-trade limits).
  • One instrument traded excellently beats five traded anxiously. Master EUR/USD or XAU/USD first.

Circuit breakers

The Global Bot ships with a max-drawdown circuit breaker that halts trading when losses hit a predefined threshold. Copy this manually: stop for the week at −4%; stop for the month at −8%. Then review the journal before resuming. Circuit breakers convert disasters into lessons.

The unforgivable sins
  • Moving a stop-loss further away "to give it room"
  • Doubling size to recover yesterday's loss (revenge trading)
  • Trading through red-folder news on manual execution
  • Risking 10% because "this one is certain"

Every blown account in history contains at least one of these. Yours doesn't have to.

06Discipline

Trading Psychology — Master Yourself First

The market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient. Your biology is wired against you — here's how to fight back.

Your brain is the enemy (and the fix)

Every open position triggers real neurological responses: losses sting roughly twice as much as equivalent gains feel good (loss aversion), and uncertainty craves resolution (which is why traders close winners early but nurse losers — the disposition effect). You cannot delete these instincts. You can only build systems that make acting on them impossible.

The four emotional states to recognise in real time

Fear

Symptom: skipping valid setups after losses, closing winners in seconds. Fix: pre-committed checklist — if it qualifies, it executes. No veto power.

Greed

Symptom: oversizing, removing TPs "to let it run", adding to losers. Fix: fixed risk %, hard exposure caps, automation of exits.

Revenge

Symptom: instant re-entry after a stop-out with bigger size. Fix: mandatory 30-minute cooldown after any loss; weekly circuit breaker.

FOMO

Symptom: chasing candles that already ran. Fix: entries only at planned levels. Missed money is not lost money.

The habits that separate professionals

  • Journal everything: screenshot, thesis, emotion rating, result. Your journal — not the market — is your best teacher. Mentorship members get theirs audited personally.
  • Fixed routine: same prep, same hours, same review. Rituals reduce decision fatigue and impulsive improvisation.
  • Detach from single outcomes: judge yourself on rule-adherence each week, not P&L. Rules followed + negative week = still a successful week.
  • Protect the machine: sleep, exercise, breaks after intense sessions. A tired trader is just a gambler with worse odds.
IMPERA Rule

If you feel compelled to deviate from your plan, the deviation itself is the signal — to close the platform. Discipline isn't a feeling. It's a decision you already made when calm.

Your First 30 Days — The Action Plan

Knowledge without a schedule evaporates. Follow this four-week ramp and finish the month with proof, not opinions.

Week 1 — Observe

  • Mark up London/NY session windows on your charts daily
  • Add EMA 8/21 + RSI(14); identify every textbook crossover (no trades yet)
  • Join the free Telegram and watch how setups are framed

Week 2 — Simulate

  • Paper-trade the Chapter 4 checklist on demo; minimum 15 logged setups
  • Calculate position size for every trade using the Chapter 5 formula
  • Journal each entry: checklist pass/fail, emotion score 1–5, outcome

Week 3 — Go Live Small

  • Trade live at 0.25–0.5% risk per trade — real money changes psychology
  • Maximum 1–2 trades/day; honour the cooldown and circuit-breaker rules
  • Review the journal every Sunday; tag your most common mistake

Week 4 — Systemise

  • Write your one-page trading plan (template below)
  • Compare demo vs live discipline honestly
  • Decide your path: continue manual, add an IMPERA bot, or accelerate with mentorship

One-page trading plan template

Fill this in and pin it above your desk
  • Instruments: ______ (start with ONE — e.g. XAU/USD or EUR/USD)
  • Sessions: London / NY — I do not trade outside ______ – ______
  • Setup: my exact trigger + confirmation conditions (from Ch. 4)
  • Risk: ____% per trade · max ____ open trades · weekly stop at −____%
  • Exits: initial SL method · TP method · trailing rule
  • Forbidden: news trading · revenge entries · moving stops · oversized lots
  • Review: journal updated after every trade · audit every Sunday

The pre-trade checklist

  • Is this a valid session (London/NY)?
  • Does trend context agree with my direction?
  • Has my exact trigger fired on a closed candle?
  • Is my confirmation indicator aligned?
  • Is spread acceptable and no red-folder news imminent?
  • Is position size calculated for ≤1% risk?
  • Am I calm? (If not — the trade is a no.)
