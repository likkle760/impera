01Foundations

Understanding the Market

Before you can win in the markets, you need to understand what moves them. Spoiler: it isn't lines on a chart. It's people, liquidity and timing.

What you're actually buying and selling

Forex is the world's largest market — over $7 trillion changes hands daily across currency pairs like EUR/USD, GBP/USD and USD/JPY. When you "buy EUR/USD" you are simultaneously buying euros and selling dollars, betting one strengthens against the other. Gold (XAU/USD) sits at the heart of it all: a safe-haven asset that reacts violently to inflation data, central bank decisions and geopolitical stress.

Pip

The smallest standard price move — 0.0001 on most pairs. EUR/USD moving 1.0850 → 1.0860 moved 10 pips.

Lot

Trade size. A standard lot = 100,000 units (~£10/pip). A mini lot (0.10) ≈ £1/pip. Micro (0.01) ≈ £0.10/pip.

Leverage

Controls size with less capital. Powerful — and the fastest way to blow up if risk isn't managed per Chapter 6.

Who moves the market

  • Central banks & governments: set interest rates and policy — the single biggest driver of long-term direction.
  • Commercial & investment banks: the interbank layer where enormous volume creates trends.
  • Hedge funds & institutions: move size in bursts, hunting liquidity — their footprints are the "smart money" patterns you'll learn to read.
  • Retail traders (you): ~5% of volume. You don't move price — you ride it. That's not a weakness, it's freedom: you can choose exactly which battles to fight.

Sessions — the rhythm of the market

The market runs 24/5, but it has a heartbeat. Volume and volatility surge during specific sessions and die off in between. Trading dead hours means wide spreads and random noise; trading the peaks means tight spreads and real moves.

Market structure — how trends are built

Every trend tells the same story in the same language: an uptrend is a sequence of higher highs and higher lows (HH/HL); a downtrend is lower highs and lower lows (LH/LL). As long as each pullback holds above the last higher low, the trend is intact. When price breaks below the last higher low, structure has shifted — that's your cue to stand down or flip bias. Reading structure first is what separates systematic traders from gamblers staring at indicators.

Support & resistance — where price remembers

Horizontal zones where price repeatedly reversed act as magnets and walls. Support below attracts buyers; resistance above attracts sellers. Two professional refinements:

  • Zones, not lines: draw them as areas (wicks included), because institutions scale in across a range, not at one magic number.
  • Role flipping: when resistance breaks convincingly, it often becomes new support — the retest of a broken level is one of the highest-probability entries in trading (Strategy 2, Chapter 4).

Liquidity — the invisible hand

Beneath obvious levels sit clusters of stop-losses. Big players hunt those pools to fill size, which is why price often spikes through a level before reversing violently ("stop hunt"). Practical consequences: place stops beyond the zone plus a buffer, never exactly at it — and expect fakeouts at obvious levels rather than being surprised by them.

Hard truth

You will never outsmart the market on information. You can, however, out-discipline 95% of participants by trading a defined plan during high-quality hours only. That alone puts you ahead of the majority.

02Price Action

Reading Price Action

Indicators lag; candles tell you what already happened right now. Learn to read raw price and every indicator becomes a confirmation, not a crutch.

Anatomy of a candle

Each candle compresses a battle into four numbers: open, high, low, close. The body shows who dominated; the wicks show where price was rejected. Long upper wick = buyers pushed up and got slammed. Long lower wick = sellers pushed down and got bought. That's supply and demand, visible in a single bar.

The candles worth memorising

  • Engulfing candle: body completely swallows the previous candle's body. Momentum handover — strongest when it appears at a key level after a pullback.
  • Pin bar (hammer/shooting star): tiny body, long tail. Price probed a level and was emphatically rejected. The tail points at the level that defended.
  • Inside bar: entire range within the previous candle. Compression — the coil before a spring (feeds Strategy 2).
  • Doji: open equals close. Indecision. Meaningless alone; meaningful at extremes of a trend.

Classic chart patterns that actually matter

Double Top / Bottom

Two failed pushes at the same zone. Signals exhaustion — enter on neckline break, target the pattern's height.

Head & Shoulders

Three peaks, middle tallest. The most reliable topping structure — again, confirmed by the break of its neckline.

Triangles & Flags

Compression patterns. Volatility contracts while energy builds; resolve in the trend direction more often than not.

Break of Structure (BOS)

Not a "pattern" but the king signal: price violating the last swing point confirms trend continuation or reversal.

Technique: multi-timeframe reading

Professionals never analyse on one timeframe. The IMPERA method stacks three:

  1. H1 — Bias: which direction does structure and the EMA trend point? This decides long-only or short-only.
  2. M15 — Setup: where is a logical level forming against that bias? Mark the zone.
  3. M5 — Trigger: drop down and wait for the precise trigger candle inside the zone. Enter there, with a tight, logical stop.
IMPERA Rule

Highest timeframe wins the argument. If M5 looks bullish but H1 structure says bearish, there is no trade. Alignment across timeframes is the setup.

03Toolkit

The Indicator Toolkit

These six tools are the complete arsenal inside IMPERA systems. Each describes either trend, momentum or volatility. Learn these deeply; ignore almost everything else.

1. Exponential Moving Averages (EMA)

The EMA weights recent price more heavily, making it responsive to fresh momentum. IMPERA systems use two: a fast EMA (8) and a slow EMA (21). When fast crosses above slow, short-term momentum has flipped bullish; below, bearish. On higher timeframes EMAs double as dynamic support/resistance — pullbacks into the rising 21 EMA are prime trend-continuation entries.

2. Relative Strength Index (RSI)

RSI measures momentum on a 0–100 scale. Above 65–70, buyers are stretched; below 30–35, sellers are exhausted. Crucially, IMPERA uses RSI as a confirmation filter, not a standalone signal: a bullish crossover is only taken when RSI shows room to run or oversold conditions turning up — filtering out exhausted entries into overextended moves.

3. MACD

The Moving Average Convergence Divergence tracks momentum shifts via the gap between two EMAs. Rising histogram = building bullish pressure; falling = bearish. Inside the Gold Bot, MACD confirms trend health on H1 while entries trigger on M15 — classic multi-timeframe confirmation.

4. Stochastic Oscillator

Stochastic locates close price within its recent range: under 20 oversold, above 80 overbought. Paired with MACD in the Gold Bot, it times reversal rotations within trending days — buying dips in uptrends instead of blindly calling tops.

5. Average True Range (ATR)

ATR answers the most important question in trading: "how far does this instrument normally move?" It converts volatility into numbers, letting stops breathe with market conditions instead of sitting at fixed distances noise routinely clips. The Gold Bot sets both SL and TP as multiples of ATR — wider in wild markets, tighter in calm ones, identical logic throughout.

6. Bollinger Bands

Bands expand and contract with volatility. Squeezes precede explosions; rides along the band confirm strong trends. The Global Bot uses band breakouts as an independent third strategy — capturing the moment a compressed range resolves.

Why only six?

Because indicators describe the same three things — trend, momentum, volatility — in different clothes. Six tools covering all three, combined with strict session timing, is everything. A chart with fifteen indicators is not more informed; it's more conflicted.

04Strategies Library

The Strategies Library

Six battle-tested ways to extract profit from the same market. Each fits a different personality and schedule — pick one primary, master it, then expand.

Strategy 1 · Trend-Pullback Continuation

The idea: trends persist far longer than expected. Don't chase the move — buy the discount. In an established uptrend (HH/HL + rising EMA 21), wait for price to pull back into the EMA zone or a prior breakout level, then enter on the first bullish trigger candle. Stop below the pullback swing; targets at prior highs or 2×ATR.

  • Best on: EUR/USD, GBP/USD, XAU/USD during London/NY
  • Killer filter: only take pullbacks against no more than 3 consecutive counter-candles — deeper than that, structure may be rolling over

Strategy 2 · Breakout & Retest

The idea: volatility cycles between compression and expansion (Bollinger squeeze, inside bars, tight ranges). When price finally breaks a well-tested boundary with momentum, two entries exist: the aggressive break itself, or the patient retest — waiting for price to return to the broken level, hold, and continue. The retest entry offers tighter stops and filters most fakeouts. Target: measured move = box height projected from the break.

  • Best on: indices, XAU/USD, post-consolidation sessions
  • Killer filter: skip if the breakout candle closes in the lower third of its range — weak conviction

Strategy 3 · Range Fading (Mean Reversion)

The idea: markets range far more than they trend — especially Asian hours and quiet Fridays. When price ping-pongs between clear horizontal boundaries, sell strength at the ceiling and buy weakness at the floor, exiting near the middle. Stochastic oversold/overbought adds timing precision. The moment a clean break occurs, flip bias or stand down.

  • Best on: EUR/GBP, AUD/NZD crosses, Asian-session ranges
  • Killer filter: require at least two clean touches of both boundaries before fading anything

Strategy 4 · Scalping

The idea: harvest many small moves on M1/M5 with tight targets and tight stops. This is the purest test of execution discipline — dozens of decisions per session, zero room for emotion. It demands a low-spread broker, fast execution and unshakeable rule-following. Done manually it is the hardest style to master; done by machine it is trivially consistent — which is why the IMPERA Scalping Bot automates this exact playbook: EMA cross trigger + RSI filter + trailing exit.

  • Needs: raw-spread account (<1 pip majors), VPS if automated, absolute focus
  • Reality check: spread+commission eats a large % of each target — costs decide profitability

Strategy 5 · Swing Trading

The idea: zoom out to H4/D1, catch multi-day swings, hold positions overnight. Fewer trades (1–3/week), bigger targets (100–300 pips), dramatically less screen time — ideal alongside a full-time job. Entries combine weekly/daily structure with H4 triggers; stops sit beyond the swing that defines the setup. This is the style taught step-by-step in the mentorship because it builds patience and perspective faster than any other.

  • Needs: tolerance for open P&L fluctuation, wider stops (smaller lots!), news awareness overnight
  • Killer filter: check the calendar — no swing positions held blind through CPI/FOMC

Strategy 6 · News-Event Trading

The idea: scheduled releases (NFP, CPI, rate decisions) create violent, liquid moves. Two honest approaches exist: the straddle (pending orders both sides seconds before release) and the post-news continuation (let the initial spike settle 5–15 minutes, then trade the retest in the direction of the surprise). Manual scalping through the spike itself is not a strategy — it's donating to liquidity providers.

  • Warning: spreads widen 5–20× during releases; stops slip. Size down accordingly
  • IMPERA stance: bots sit out red-folder events by design; manual traders should too until experienced

05Core System

The IMPERA Entry Model

No secrets held back. These are the precise entry, exit and management rules our own systems execute — the confluence engine behind the bots.

The philosophy: precision over prediction

Amateurs try to be right. Professionals try to be consistent. You cannot control where price goes next — you completely control whether you follow your rules. An edge simply means that over many trades, your average win outweighs your average loss after costs:

Expectancy = (Win% × AvgWin) − (Loss% × AvgLoss)
// e.g. 55% × £150 − 45% × £100 = +£37.50 per trade
// A positive number, repeated hundreds of times, is the whole business model.

The setup (long example)

  1. Trend context: price above rising 21 EMA on M15/H1 — longs only.
  2. Trigger: 8 EMA crosses above 21 EMA on M5 (fresh cross, closed candles).
  3. Confirmation: RSI(14) below 35 turning up (pullback exhausted) or driving through 50 with momentum.
  4. Session gate: 08:00–20:00 server time, London + New York only.
  5. Spread check: below maximum threshold — if crossing the spread costs too much, skip.
Mirror image for shorts

Price below falling 21 EMA + 8 EMA crosses below + RSI above 65 rolling over + valid session + clean spread. Same discipline, opposite direction.

Exits — where trades are won

  • Initial stop: fixed-pips version for majors (default 20), or ATR-based (Gold Bot: SL ≈ 1.5×ATR, TP ≈ 2.5×ATR) so distance adapts to live volatility.
  • Take profit: structural or ATR-projected targets — never widened mid-trade on hope.
  • Trailing stop: once profit exceeds ~10 pips, trail by 5-pip steps. Winners get room early, then get locked in.
  • Exposure cap: max 3 concurrent positions (Scalping Bot), 2 (Gold Bot), 5 (Global Bot).

Risk-reward: the arithmetic of being wrong

Here's the mental shift that changes careers: with disciplined 1:2 risk-reward, you can lose more trades than you win and compound aggressively. Risk £100 to make £200 and a 40% win rate still prints money. Chasing 90% win rates with 1:5 risk is how accounts die quietly.

06Capital

Risk Management — Your Real Edge

Strategy decides how you win. Risk management decides whether you survive long enough to win at all. This is the most important chapter in the book.

The 1% commandment

Never risk more than 1% of your account on a single trade (2% absolute ceiling). At 1%, even a catastrophic 10-loss streak costs ~9.6% — painful but fully recoverable. At 10%, the same streak erases 65% of the account and demands a +186% climb just to break even. Survival math is brutal and non-negotiable:

DrawdownGain needed to recoverVerdict
−5%+5.3%Routine
−10%+11%Recoverable
−25%+33%Serious damage
−50%+100%Career-ending
−75%+300%Game over

Position sizing — the only formula you need

Lot Size = (Account × Risk%) ÷ (Stop Loss in Pips × Pip Value)
// £2,000 account · 1% risk · 20-pip stop · £10/pip per lot (majors)
Lot Size = (£2,000 × 0.01) ÷ (20 × £10) = 0.01 lots

Notice what this means: the stop distance determines the lot size — never the other way round. Choose invalidation first (structure/ATR), then let maths dictate size. This is exactly how the bots' dynamic lot sizing behaves: constant risk, balance-scaling exposure.

Diversification & correlation

  • EUR/USD and GBP/USD are ~85% correlated — long both and you've doubled real risk, not diversified it.
  • Cap total open risk across all positions (bots enforce this with concurrent-trade limits).
  • One instrument traded excellently beats five traded anxiously. Master XAU/USD or EUR/USD first.

Circuit breakers

The Global Bot ships with a max-drawdown circuit breaker halting trading at a predefined loss threshold. Copy it manually: stop for the week at −4%, stop for the month at −8%. Review the journal before resuming. Circuit breakers convert disasters into lessons.

The unforgivable sins
  • Moving a stop-loss further away "to give it room"
  • Doubling size to recover yesterday's loss (revenge trading)
  • Trading through red-folder news on manual execution
  • Risking 10% because "this one is certain"

Every blown account in history contains at least one of these. Yours doesn't have to.

07Discipline

Trading Psychology

The market is a device for transferring money from the impatient to the patient. Your biology is wired against you — here's the counter-system.

Your brain is the enemy (and the fix)

Every open position triggers real neurological responses: losses sting roughly twice as much as equivalent gains feel good (loss aversion), and uncertainty craves resolution — which is why traders close winners instantly but nurse losers for weeks (the disposition effect). You cannot delete these instincts. You can build systems that make acting on them impossible.

The four emotional states

Fear

Symptom: skipping valid setups after losses, closing winners in seconds. Fix: pre-committed checklist — if it qualifies, it executes. No veto power.

Greed

Symptom: oversizing, removing TPs "to let it run", adding to losers. Fix: fixed risk %, hard exposure caps, automated exits.

Revenge

Symptom: instant re-entry after a stop-out with bigger size. Fix: mandatory 30-minute cooldown after any loss; weekly circuit breaker.

FOMO

Symptom: chasing candles that already ran. Fix: entries only at planned levels. Missed money is not lost money.

The habits of professionals

  • Journal everything: screenshot, thesis, emotion rating, result. Your journal — not the market — is your best teacher. Mentorship members get theirs audited personally.
  • Fixed routine: same prep, same hours, same review. Rituals reduce decision fatigue.
  • Judge process, not outcomes: rules followed + negative week = successful week.
  • Protect the machine: sleep, exercise, breaks after intense sessions. A tired trader is a gambler with worse odds.
IMPERA Rule

If you feel compelled to deviate from your plan, the deviation itself is the signal — to close the platform. Discipline isn't a feeling. It's a decision you already made when calm.
